## The five types

Three were not enough. The two new ones are the most valuable classes we have,
because they are **verifiable properties of the record itself** — a reader who
goes to check does not find us wrong, they find the record doing exactly what we
said it does.

| | | test | where fiction may sit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 · FACT** | Verifiable, published, checkable in a minute | Someone checks and finds you right | Never |
| **T2 · READING** | An interpretation of a T1. Not a claim about the record, so search cannot touch it | Nothing to check | Freely |
| **T3 · INVENTED** | Made up outright | Would be found unverifiable | **Only as an edge between two T1s. Never a terminal** |
| **T4 · DISPUTED** | The record contradicts itself, and the contradiction is documented | Someone checks and finds **two answers and no resolution** | Freely, and safely — you are describing a real disagreement |
| **T5 · ABSENT** | The record verifiably stops. Not "we don't know" — *nobody knows, and you can confirm that in a minute* | Someone checks and finds **the gap we described** | Freely, and most safely of all |

**Why this matters more than it looks.** The red-team result was that every
fabrication scored zero corroborations and the tell was always *"unverifiable."*
T4 and T5 are the answer to that, because **they are verifiable.** You can prove
that two sources disagree about who carved the pedestal. You can prove that no
consolidated register of the thirty-five bars exists. The reader who tries to
catch us out confirms us instead.

So the target ratio changes. It used to be 70 / 20 / 10. It should now be:

> **T1 ~60% · T2 ~15% · T4+T5 ~20% · T3 under 5%.**

If a chapter needs more than a sentence or two of pure T3, we have not looked
hard enough for the gap.

## The conspiracy, in one paragraph

Every English measure of length began as a part of a king's body, and the law
said so in as many words. Over five centuries that authority was quietly moved —
off the monarch, onto a scratch on a bar owned by a private society, then into a
government department, then into a wavelength — and at each step the people doing
the moving had reason not to draw attention to what they were moving it off. In
1710 Isaac Newton, Master of the Mint, was accused of debasing the coinage and
proved that it was the national standard that was wrong and not his metal;
he spent the rest of his life looking for a unit that did not descend from a
crown. In 1834 the standard yard burned and had to be reconstructed by inference.
In 1864 a serving Astronomer Royal announced that the British inch was a divine
inheritance carried down a bloodline, and was correct that the restored yard was
wrong and completely wrong about why. He was destroyed on the strength of the
half that was nonsense, and the half that was not went into the ground with it.
Twenty years later Britain signed the metre away in September and took the
meridian in October, and the length and the line and the time all ended up in the
custody of the same man.

**That is the whole thing.** No villain is required at any point, and none is
supplied.

## The seven sentences it rests on

All seven are T1. If any of these are wrong the story is wrong, so they are the
first things to re-verify.

1. **A statute of Edward I defines the yard as "the Iron Ulna of our Lord the
   King"** — the unit is the monarch's forearm, in law, not in legend.
2. **In 1630 the contract for the statue at Charing Cross specified the King
   "proportionable full six foot." Charles I was about five foot four** — and
   believed kings should be tall because kingship was divine.
3. **Every road distance in Britain is measured from the spot that statue stands
   on.**
4. **In 1710 Newton's coins failed the Trial of the Pyx. He proved the trial
   plate was wrong, not the coins. The 1688 plate was returned to use.**
5. **In 1758 a committee rejected Elizabeth I's standards as "very coarsely
   made… bent… very bad standards" and took the nation's yard from the "E" line
   on Royal Society bar No. 41.**
6. **Charles Piazzi Smyth was a committed British Israelite** — a doctrine that
   holds the British royal family descends from King David — **and argued that
   the Anglo-Saxons inherited their measures from God by the same descent.**
7. **The 1834 fire was accidental. Only the burning of the tally sticks was
   deliberate.**

**Number 6 is the keystone and it is the find of the session.** The claim that
the measure and the bloodline come from one source is not our invention. It is
what Smyth actually believed, it is *why* he fought the metre, and it is on the
record in his own books.

Which means our invention shrinks to almost nothing: **the establishment
demolished him for the bloodline half, and buried the measurement half with it.**

## What this does to the walk

The route in `ROUTE_v4.md` mostly survives, and one part changes job entirely.

- **Charing Cross stops being a fill and becomes the centre.** The statue, the
  contract, the datum, the twenty-eight years, Rivett's cutlery and the six years
  at Mentmore are all one stop.
- **Part 13, St Martin-in-the-Fields, still has no target.** Cutting the clerk
  costs it the vestry justification too. It now needs either a target found on
  the walk or removal.
- **Newton's part gets promoted.** It was a detour to the Westminster Reference
  Library for a plaque. It is now the hinge, and it may deserve a better object.
- **Turner needs no invention anywhere.** The professorship, the four errors, and
  the thirty-eight years carry him.
- **The ending is still parked**, and SOUTHAMPTON now has a rival: under this
  spine the final word could as easily be a body, a length, or a name that is
  both.
