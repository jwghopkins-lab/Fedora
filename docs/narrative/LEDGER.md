recovered in a line. This supersedes the scattered lists in `NARRATIVE.md`,

## How to read it

**Type**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | **FACT** | Verifiable, published, checkable in a minute. The only thing a reader may be asked to stand on |
| **T2** | **READING** | An interpretation of a T1. Not a claim about the record, so search cannot touch it |
| **T3** | **INVENTED** | Made up. Only ever an edge between two T1s, never a terminal |
| **T4** | **DISPUTED** | The record contradicts itself, and the contradiction is documented. A reader who checks finds two answers and no resolution |
| **T5** | **ABSENT** | The record verifiably stops. Not "we don't know" — *nobody knows, and you can confirm that in a minute* |

T4 and T5 are the two safest places for fiction, because they are **verifiable
properties of the record**. Someone trying to catch us out confirms us instead.

| **✕** | **CUT** — removed, with the reason. Recoverable |
| **★** | **NEW** — proposed this round, not yet placed |

## The new suggestions, argued

Fourteen new rows. In order of how much they are worth.

### 1 · The King's Champion — **the best of them**
**1377 hereditary · 1821 last performed · 1838 dropped.** An office whose entire
function was *to challenge anyone who denied the monarch's entitlement*, held by
one family for six hundred years, discontinued in **1838** — the same year Airy's
commission sat down to reconstruct the yard from inference, and the year Turner
resigned the chair of geometry.

The ceremony for disputing the succession and the object defining the measure
both stopped being physical things in the same year. Neither half needs a word of
invention.

### 2 · The regalia melted into coin, 1649
**"Totally broken."** The stones sold, the gold melted **at the Tower Mint** and
struck into coins stamped *Commonwealth of England*. Alfred's crown and Queen
Edith's crown among them.

The crown physically became money, in the building Newton would run fifty years
later, prosecuting men for turning money into false royal metal. And it gives us
a third substituted object to sit beside the statue and the Stone.

### 3 · *Refurbished, Recycled or Replaced?*
**1661, St Edward's Crown.** Whether the crown used at every coronation since
contains any of the medieval original is **a published scholarly question with
that title.** A T4 of the highest grade: a reader who checks finds historians
arguing about exactly what we said they argue about.

Three objects now carry the same unresolved question — the crown, the stone, the
statue — and all three disputes are real.

### 4 · Ten regicides at Charing Cross, October 1660
Hanged, drawn and quartered **on the spot the statue now stands on**, fifteen
years before it was put there. The statue looks down Whitehall to where he was
executed. **And every road distance in Britain is measured from that ground.**

### 5 · The White Ship, 1120
Henry I — the king whose arm is the yard — lost his heir and his succession in
one night, and the country fell into nineteen years of civil war over who was
really entitled. **The measure and the disputed succession arrive together, at
the very top of the story.**

### 6 · Article 17, 1707
The Treaty of Union **abolished Scottish weights and measures**. A century and a
half later the Astronomer Royal *for Scotland* defended imperial measure as a
divine inheritance. Free irony, and it explains Smyth's position better than any
psychology could.

### 7 · 1837, the crown forks by law
Victoria's accession ends the personal union with Hanover under Salic law and
the Hanoverian crown goes to Ernest Augustus. **A real, legal, documented fork in
the line, inside our period** — which makes the premise ordinary rather than
exotic.

### 8 · Charles II dates his reign from 1649 ⚠
Eleven years of legal fiction, on the record, about royal continuity. Verify the
phrasing before using it.

### 9–14 · The smaller ones
The Elizabethan yard being 0.01 in short · the 1884 vote breakdown (San Domingo
against; France and Brazil abstaining) · the statue facing its own execution site
· the 1936 abdication · the Turner Bequest · **and the 1742 Royal Society yard
question**, which is either a good T1 or a good T4 and needs resolving either way.

## What the cut material is worth now

**Recover one thing: the 222 yards.** Two Charing Crosses, a furlong apart, and
the nation measures from one of them. It belongs to the ARM thread.

**Reconsider one thing: Belsky.** Cut for good reasons, but the twentieth century
is still thin, and *a sculptor sealing a sixpence into a bust in 1967 and a jam
jar into a leg in 1983* is the custodian chain continuing into living memory. If
the Mentmore row and the Stone of Scone rows carry the century well enough, leave
him cut. If they do not, he is the obvious repair.

**Leave the rest cut.** The light thread has nothing to attach to now that the
metre-becomes-light row has no neighbours. The Cenotaph and the lions were
stating a thesis that the *Temeraire*'s thirty-eight years and Nelson's fourteen
feet six inches state better. Napier, Dryden, Terriss and the plinth alternatives
were always decoration.
