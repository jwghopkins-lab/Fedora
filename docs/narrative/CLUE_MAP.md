# GROUND TRUTH — chapters to pavement

Every chapter in `SPINE.md`, mapped to what can be physically stood in front of
in central London, and what could be measured or found there.

Two columns matter: **carrier** (an object that already exists and can hold a
clue) and **find** (something we do not yet know and must go and get).

**Nothing below marked ⚑ has been verified.** They are candidates from
knowledge, not from the pavement, and several would be significant additions to
the route if they check out.

---

## The five candidates that are not in the route and probably should be

### ⚑ 1. The Jewel Tower, Old Palace Yard

**This may be the single most on-theme building in London and we are walking
past it.**

- It is one of only two buildings that **survived the 1834 fire**.
- The **Standard Weights and Measures Department worked in it from 1869 to
  1938.**
- It left because **road traffic vibration made precise measurement
  impossible** — the city physically shook the standards out of their own
  building.

It is about two hundred metres from Parliament Square, which is part 2. Chapter
2 (the fire), chapter 4 (the rebuilding) and the whole modern collapse of
physical standards all have an address, and it is on the walk already.

*Find:* is there an inscription, a date stone, anything readable from outside?
Is the site interpretation board any use? Opening hours and cost.

### ⚑ 2. Newton's house, St Martin's Street

Newton lived off Leicester Square in the 1710s–20s, a few minutes from
Trafalgar Square and directly between Act 2 and Act 3.

If a plaque survives, chapter 7 — **the hinge of the book** — gets a physical
address on the route, and the Newton thread stops being purely documentary.

*Find:* does the plaque exist, what does it say, exactly where, and is the
street walkable between Trafalgar and Covent Garden without a detour?

### ⚑ 3. Parish boundary marks

Small iron or stone plates, initials and a date, set into walls at the parish
line. St Martin-in-the-Fields and St Paul's Covent Garden had a boundary
defined as *"40 foote without the ... bricke wall"* and it ran through what is
now the route.

Perfect clue material: unmissable once pointed at, invisible otherwise, and
**they are the physical residue of beating the bounds** — chapter 11 standing
in the street.

*Find:* how many survive on or near St Martin's Lane, Bedford Street, Maiden
Lane and the piazza. Photograph the lettering. This is the highest-value
unknown on the list.

### ⚑ 4. The Royal Society, Carlton House Terrace

Where the pyramid paper was rejected in 1874 and where Smyth resigned his
Fellowship in 1888. Just off the Mall, near Admiralty Arch, i.e. beside the
Act 1 → Act 2 walk.

*Find:* is it visible and identifiable from the pavement, and does the walk
pass it without a detour? Note the Society was not there in 1874 — check where
it was then, because that address may be the better one.

### ⚑ 5. The Parliamentary Copy in the New Palace

One of the four copies was **walled into the New Palace at Westminster**. If
its position is recorded and visible, a team can stand in front of a wall with
a standard yard inside it — which rhymes exactly with Belsky's bust and the
whole promise/proof structure of D7.

*Find:* where, and can it be seen or pointed at from a public footway.

## Things to measure, in priority order

1. **The terrace brass, in full, with a tape in shot.** Nothing physical can be
   built until this exists. It gates the cord, and the cord gates the ending.
2. **Every cord anchor and landing.** Distances are *discovered, not designed* —
   that is the whole security argument. Each needs a photograph and a
   description unambiguous enough to use in the dark.
3. **Parish boundary marks.** Unknown quantity, potentially the best clue
   material on the route, and it would put chapter 11 on the pavement.
4. **Southampton Street / Maiden Lane geometry**, and the finish venue.
5. **The Jewel Tower**: legibility, hours, and whether it earns a part.
6. **Newton's plaque**: exists or not, and where.
7. **Cenotaph end faces** — already on the list, and the clue may not survive
   the safety check.
8. **The lions**: dog or cat. Settle it with a photograph.

## One conflict worth naming now

If the book is ever written and published, **it contains the walk's answers.**
Every chapter above points at the object a clue is built on, and chapter 20
names the final word.

There are only three ways out, and they should be chosen deliberately rather
than discovered late:

1. **The book comes after.** The walk runs first, for however many cohorts, and
   the book is the record of it. Simplest, and it makes the book's ending true.
2. **They share the story and not the route.** The book uses the same history
   and different objects — the history is public property, the specific
   pavement is not.
3. **The book *is* the diary.** Not a novel about the walk but the artifact
   itself, published as a facsimile. Then the answers are supposed to be in it,
   because that is what it is for.

This is asked as **D19**.
