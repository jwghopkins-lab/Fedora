# Westminster — candidate stops after the Jewel Tower

Six ideas for one or two more stops between the Jewel Tower and Trafalgar
Square. Each is free, outdoors, within 300 m of the Jewel Tower, and works with
the location-then-camera pattern or camera alone. Types as in `LEDGER.md`.
⚠ = verify before writing.

The two beats this section has to land: **what really burned in 1834, and
what we say did** · **Newton's notes, and the number that leads to the cubit.**

---

## A · The hall that did not burn
**Westminster Hall, north front · from St Margaret Street / Parliament Square**
· 51.5003, −0.1265 · free · 150 m north

**Mechanic.** Gate at the pavement opposite; camera stencil of the great north
window and gable. One question, three guesses.

**True.** The hall survived 1834 while everything round it went. The fire began
with the burning of the Exchequer's tally sticks in the Lords' furnaces. It
destroyed the Imperial Standard Yard of 1760 and the pound. **Newton sat in the
building that burned:** he was MP for Cambridge University in 1689 and 1701,
in St Stephen's Chapel, the Commons' chamber until the fire. Dickens, 1855:
*"official routine required that they never should be [useful], and so the
order went forth that they were to be privately and confidentially burnt."* ⚠
exact wording.

**The fiction hook.** The truth is already stranger than the lie: the sticks
were burned on purpose, by order, in a furnace under the House of Lords, and
the fire got out. Arthur's addition is small and unfalsifiable: *not everything
that was lost was in the building that night.*

**Question.** "What did they burn to start it?" → **STICKS** · **S**.

**For the plot.** This is the "what burned" reveal, straight. Strong, obvious,
and every fact is on a plaque or in Hansard.

## B · The tower built so nothing would burn again
**Victoria Tower · seen from the Jewel Tower forecourt or Abingdon Street**
· 51.4984, −0.1248 · free · **zero walking: it is across the road**

**Mechanic.** No new gate. Camera only, stencil of the tower's silhouette, one
of the most distinctive outlines in London.

**True.** Built after the fire as a **fireproof repository for the records of
Parliament**, because the Commons' records had burned and the Lords' largely
survived. It held the Parliamentary Archives until their move to Kew ⚠ dates.
**Arthur was an archivist.** This is his building.

**The fiction hook.** T5, clean: a document that the fire-proof tower was built
to hold, that went in, and that no catalogue lists. Arthur worked there; the
diary says what he could not find.

**For the plot.** The fire beat from the other side — not what burned but what
they built so it could never happen again, and what still went missing anyway.
It is also the only stop that ties Arthur himself to the ground.

## C · The yard in the wall
**The Palace of Westminster · Old Palace Yard / St Stephen's entrance**
· 51.4995, −0.1257 · free · 100 m north

**Mechanic.** Camera at the façade; stencil of St Stephen's porch. Or no stop
at all — one line in another stop's after-text.

**True.** When the standards were remade in 1855, four Parliamentary Copies
were cast. One went to the Mint, one to the Royal Society, one to Greenwich,
and **one was bricked into the wall of the Palace of Westminster**, taken out
and compared every twenty years. ⚠ exact location inside the building.

**The fiction hook.** None needed. A yard of bronze is inside that wall and
the player cannot reach it.

**For the plot.** **The best bridge to Trafalgar Square there is:** *the copy
in the wall you cannot touch; the copies on the terrace you can.* If it is not
its own stop, it is the last line of the Westminster section.

## D · The clock the Astronomer Royal set
**Elizabeth Tower · from Parliament Square / Bridge Street**
· 51.5007, −0.1246 · free · 250 m north

**Mechanic.** Camera stencil of the clock face. Optional: a question that
needs the hour to strike.

**True.** Built on the ashes. **Airy**, as Astronomer Royal, set its accuracy:
the first stroke of each hour true to within a second, checked twice a day by
telegraph to Greenwich. Its rate is trimmed by stacking old pennies on the
pendulum, one penny ≈ 0.4 seconds a day.

**The fiction hook.** None. Airy's name enters the game here, on a tower that
exists because of the fire, twenty years before he declines to hear Smyth.

**Question.** "What do they stack on the pendulum?" → **PENNIES**.

**For the plot.** Introduces the antagonist by name, and the time thread, at
the moment the player is looking at the most famous clock in the world. Pays
off at Burlington House.

## E · He is fifty feet behind this wall
**Westminster Abbey, north transept · Parliament Square side**
· 51.4996, −0.1276 · free exterior · 200 m north-west

**Mechanic.** Gate at the north door; camera stencil of the rose window.
Text, no question, or a question on the number.

**True.** Newton was buried here on 28 March 1727, pallbearers the Lord
Chancellor, two dukes and three earls; Voltaire watched and said England
buried its mathematicians like kings. ⚠ names. The west towers everyone
photographs were finished in 1745 — he never saw them. **And the number:** his
*Dissertation upon the Sacred Cubit of the Jews*, published after his death in
1737, puts the sacred cubit at about **25 English inches** ⚠ exact figure —
**the figure Smyth would take as the pyramid cubit a century and a half
later.** Newton wanted it to rebuild Solomon's Temple on paper and to fix the
size of the earth for his gravitation.

**The fiction hook.** The dissertation is real and dull and in Latin. Arthur's
diary carries the number and one line Newton did not print.

**For the plot.** This is the "Newton's notes lead to the cubit" beat, done
standing outside the wall he is behind. No ticket, and the refusal of entry is
part of the feeling.

## F · The standard that was wrong
**The Pyx Chamber, Westminster Abbey · from Dean's Yard or Abingdon Street**
· 51.4988, −0.1275 · free exterior · ⚠ which vantage sees the Chapter House

**Mechanic.** Camera stencil of the Chapter House buttresses (the Pyx Chamber
is the undercroft beside it). One question.

**True.** The Pyx Chamber was the royal treasury and held the **trial plates**
of gold and silver — the standards against which the coinage was tested at
the Trial of the Pyx. **Newton, as Master of the Mint, was tested against
them.** In 1710 the jury found his gold below standard; **Newton argued the
trial plate itself was wrong, and he was right** — it had been made too fine.
⚠ year and detail, Craig *Newton at the Mint*. The Trial of the Pyx still
happens every year.

**The fiction hook.** None needed, and that is the point. **The man who
checked the cubit for twenty years also checked the King's standard and found
the standard was the thing that was wrong.** Same man, same habit, same
result. The diary only has to say so.

**Question.** "Newton said it was wrong. What was it?" → **THE PLATE** or
**STANDARD** · **S**.

**For the plot.** The whole book's argument in one Westminster fact: the
standard can be wrong, and the person who says so is the one who measured.
Sets up Smyth by inversion — Newton checked and was right; Smyth believed and
was wrong; the walk is about knowing which you are doing.

## Recommendation

**First choice: B then F.** Jewel Tower → Victoria Tower (camera only, no
walking, Arthur's own building, what they built after the fire) → the Pyx
Chamber (Newton proves the standard wrong) → Trafalgar Square, with **C's
line as the section's last words**: *one copy is in that wall; four more are
on a terrace a mile north.* Three stops, two reveals, one question, the letter
S, and the section ends pointing at the cord.

**Second choice: A then E.** The straighter telling: the hall that survived
and the sticks that started it, then Newton behind the Abbey wall with the
number. More familiar, more sight-seeing, still all true.

**D is worth keeping for later.** Airy's name should enter the game somewhere
before Burlington House, and the clock is the natural place; it can be a
passing beat on the walk north rather than a stop.

**What to verify for whichever pair you pick:** the 1710 Pyx dispute; Newton's
cubit figure; the Victoria Tower archive dates; the exact position of the
walled copy.

# Part 2 — the Egyptian link: clue 2 and the road to Trafalgar

Constraints now fixed: A is referenced, C is referenced, B adds little, **D is
clue 3 and brings in Airy**, the Pyx is a red herring. Clue 2 must be
reachable, camera-able, and carry the Egyptian thread. The lead-in text is
Arthur's copy of Newton's private working papers on the pyramid and the cubit.

Everything below was checked this session unless marked ⚠.

## Two facts that change the shape

**Newton got the cubit right, from a book.** His *Dissertation upon the Sacred
Cubit of the Jews* (published 1737 in Greaves's *Miscellaneous Works*) takes
John Greaves's 1639 measurements of the King's Chamber and determines the
cubit of Memphis at **1719/1000 of an English foot — 20.63 inches.** The
modern figure for the royal cubit is about 20.6 inches. **He was within a
millimetre, in the 1680s, never having seen a stone of Egypt.** The full text
is online at the Newton Project, Oxford.

**The Victorians buried a set of imperial weights under an Egyptian obelisk.**
The time capsule sealed in the pedestal of Cleopatra's Needle on 12 September
1878 contains, on the record: **a set of imperial weights**, a complete set of
British coins, a rupee, twelve photographs of "the best-looking English women
of the day", a box of hairpins, cigars, a razor, a Bradshaw, a Whitaker's, a map
of London, and the Bible in several languages. The measures and the coins are
in the same jar. **The red herring is physically buried beside the real
thing.**

## Ideas

### 1 · The pyramid on his tomb
**Westminster Abbey, north transept, from Parliament Square** · 51.4996,
−0.1276 · free · 250 m from the Jewel Tower

**Visual.** The rose window: a circle with tracery, the most stencil-friendly
shape in Westminster. ⚠ Check the north front is not scaffolded.

**True.** Newton lies in the nave against the choir screen, fifty feet behind
that wall. His monument (Kent and Rysbrack, unveiled 1731) shows him reclining
beneath a celestial globe **on a pyramid**. On the sarcophagus relief, boys
handle a telescope and a prism, **and one weighs the sun and the planets on a
steelyard.** The men who buried him put a pyramid and a balance on his tomb.

**The lead-in lands here.** The diary text about Arthur's copy of the working
papers is read standing outside the wall he is behind, with the pyramid in the
diary's sketch and the number on the page.

**Question.** The diary page carries Greaves's chamber length and Newton's rule.
"How many thousandths of a foot?" → **1719.** A number the player reads off
the working, that the app never explains until Bloomsbury.

### 2 · The burnt pages
**Not a place. A fact for the diary at idea 1.**

**True.** In December 2020 Sotheby's sold **three scorched pages of Newton's
own notes on the Great Pyramid**, survivors of a fire said to have been
started by his dog Diamond knocking over a candle, for **£378,000**, to a
private buyer. They argue the pyramid was designed around the royal cubit.

**Why it matters.** The lead-in says *the only public copy*. That is now
literally true: the originals are in a private collection since 2020. Newton's
pyramid notes burned; the standards burned; Arthur's copy is what is left.
**Nothing invented, and it is the spine of the whole conceit.**

### 3 · The weights under the obelisk
**Cleopatra's Needle, Victoria Embankment** · 51.5085, −0.1203 · free, always
· on the walk from Big Ben to Trafalgar Square

**Visual.** The obelisk and one sphinx. A silhouette nobody could mistake.

**True.** Thutmose III, c. 1450 BC, from Heliopolis. Erected 12 September
1878. Six men died bringing it through the Bay of Biscay in 1877 ⚠ number.
The two bronze sphinxes by Vulliamy **face inward, towards the obelisk, which
is backwards** — Egyptian sphinxes guard outward. A 1917 bomb scarred the
right-hand sphinx and pedestal; a plaque says so. And beneath it, the imperial
weights, in a jar with the coins.

**The fiction hook.** None needed. *The empire set a copy of its measures under
an Egyptian stone, facing its guardians the wrong way, and forgot.* The diary
notes the coins in the same jar and crosses them out.

**Question.** "Which way do the sphinxes face?" → **INWARD** / **TOWARDS IT**.
Or the year on the shrapnel plaque → **1917.**

### 4 · The sphinx on the bench
**Victoria Embankment benches, from Westminster Bridge northward** · free ·
100 m from Big Ben

**Visual.** One cast-iron winged sphinx armrest, at arm's length. A close-range
camera target, the opposite of a building: it tests the match on something
small and tests the player's hands.

**True.** Benches by Z. D. Berry & Son, placed **1877 — a year before the
Needle arrived**, with winged sphinxes and camels for arms. London dressed the
river in Egypt before the stone got here.

**Use.** A passing camera beat on clue 3, or the first thing the player is
asked to find on leaving Big Ben for the Needle.

### 5 · The number that carries
**Not a place. The puzzle thread.**

At idea 1 the player reads **1719** from the diary. At the Petrie Museum the
cubit rods in the case are 20.6 inches long, and the stencil fails by a digit.
The app says nothing about the connection until the Bloomsbury after-text:
*"The number he got from a book in 1680 is the length of the stick in front of
you."* Newton's number, Petrie's stick, one sentence, two chapters apart.

### 6 · The Nile at Trafalgar
**Nelson's Column, the base reliefs** · the bridge into the next chapter

**True.** One of the four bronze reliefs is the Battle of the Nile, 1798 ⚠
which face. The Rosetta Stone came to London as spoils of that campaign in
1802. **The public standards on the terrace sit fifty metres from a relief of
the battle that brought Egypt's key to London.** The Egyptian thread does not
end at the Needle; it walks into the next chapter with the player.

walk does.**

**The honest caveat.** Clue 2's *visual* is Gothic; the Egyptian *visual* comes
at clue 4. If an Egyptian object is wanted in the camera at clue 2 itself, the
only one inside Westminster is the sphinx bench, and it is better spent on the
walk to the Needle.

**Field checks:** the Abbey north front for scaffolding · the nearest sphinx
bench to Westminster Bridge · the Needle's plaque wording · which face of the
