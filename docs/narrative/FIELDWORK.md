# GROUND TRUTH — the field list

Everything the design now depends on that nobody has stood in front of.
Ordered as **one walk**, Parliament Square to Maiden Lane, so it is a single
trip rather than five.

Nothing here is blocked by D11. This is the work that can happen now.

**Take:** a tape measure, a phone with the app open, and something to write on.
**Photograph everything.** Wording, not gist — several clues turn on exact
inscriptions and my text currently assumes them.

---

## 1. Parliament Square — part 2, gated

- [ ] **Find Ray Strachey on the plinth.** Which face, how high, is the name
      legible standing up or does it need crouching. Photograph the panel.
- [ ] **Gate check.** Radius is 300 m. Note the accuracy figure the phone
      reports, not just whether it passed — the pass rule credits accuracy up
      to 150 m and I want to see real numbers.

## 2. Whitehall — the Cenotaph — part 3 **← the one that might not survive**

- [ ] **Confirm MCMXIV and MCMXIX on the end faces**, and MCMXXXIX / MCMXLV on
      the sides. Photograph all four.
- [ ] **Can you safely read both ends from the pavement?** The Cenotaph is on
      an island in a live carriageway. The clue says *walk round it*. If that
      means crossing Whitehall twice, or standing in the road, **the clue is
      unsafe and has to be rewritten** — the answer would have to come from one
      face, or from something else entirely. Decide this on site; it is the
      single biggest risk in the new text.
- [ ] Is the 1919 date legible at all, or is it weathered?

## 3. King Charles Street → Horse Guards Road

- [ ] **Are the steps open?** This is a security-sensitive stretch and closures
      happen. If it is shut, the route needs an alternative and part 3's
      explainer changes.
- [ ] Time it.

## 4. Mountbatten Green — part 4

- [ ] **Right hand: binoculars? Left hand: empty?** Photograph both hands. The
      whole clue rests on this and I have it from a description, not a picture.
- [ ] Can the public get close enough to see the hands clearly, or is it
      fenced/set back?
- [ ] Photograph the plinth inscription in full.

## 5. Trafalgar Square — the north terrace — **the most important item on this list**

Parts 12–16 sit here, and **the cord cannot be cut until this is done** (D10:
knot positions are measured against the actual brass, never calculated, because
the 2003 rebuild moved the plates and no drawing is authoritative).

- [ ] **Photograph the entire brass run, in sequence, with a tape in shot.**
      Every marker, its inscription, and the distance between them.
- [ ] Confirm it really is 100 ft in 10 ft steps. Confirm the chain (66 ft),
      the perch, the yard and the foot, and where each one physically sits.
- [ ] **Measure brass → Cunningham bust.** Part 16's explainer says "twenty
      feet from the public standards". That number is mine. Correct it.
- [ ] **Part 12 is still in collect mode** — *"Exactly thirty-five levels.
      Beneath your feet:"* — we have never known the intended answer. Work it
      out standing on it, or the part stays a collector.
- [ ] Is the terrace ever roped off, cleaned, or used for events? A closed
      terrace is a dead hunt.

## 6. The lions — part 6, gated

- [ ] **Photograph the paws and settle it: dog or cat?** The script says
      dog-like and "his own dog"; published accounts say a domestic cat's paws
      and concave backs. One of them is wrong and it is exactly what a player
      checks while standing there.
- [ ] Gate check at 250 m; note the accuracy figure.

## 7. St Martin-in-the-Fields — part 18

- [ ] **Is the east window visible, and when?** It is above the altar. Is the
      church open on the day and at the hour teams will arrive, and is there a
      service running?
- [ ] **Is it visible from outside?** Sources say the lit ellipse reads
      internally *and* externally. If that is true from the churchyard, we have
      a fallback and the clue is safe whatever the church is doing.
- [ ] Photograph it from both.

## 8. St Martin's Lane — parts 19–21

- [ ] Gate check outside Mr Fogg's at 200 m.
- [ ] St Martin's Court and Goodwin's Court: already walked, no action.

## 9. Maiden Lane — part 22

- [ ] **Find all three plaques** — Turner, Voltaire, Terriss. Photograph each,
      record the exact wording and which building it is on.
- [ ] Confirm Turner's gives **1775**.
- [ ] Is the Voltaire plaque legible from the pavement, and is it definitely on
      this lane?

## 10. The finish — only if D11 goes the rendezvous way

- [ ] Scout somewhere within five minutes of Maiden Lane where one person can
      sit for two or three hours while teams arrive at different times.
      Indoors or weatherproof. Nameable in one word.
