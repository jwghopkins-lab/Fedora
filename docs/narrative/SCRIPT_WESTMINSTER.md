## Stop 2 · Location two → Westminster Abbey

**Gate:** the north transept door, Parliament Square side · 51.4996, −0.1276 ·
radius 40 m · ⚠ confirm on site.
**Compass** beyond 500 m, **distance** all the way, as before.
**Camera:** stencil of the **rose window** above the north door. *[Needs a
photo from the pavement opposite, phone held level, whole window in frame. A
geometric placeholder is possible but a photo is better; the tracery is what
the match will lock on to.]*

### Body — read on opening

> The only public copy of Sir Isaac Newton's private notes on the Great
> Pyramid and the sacred cubit.
>
> Arthur's recovered notes contain Newton's private working papers from the
> 1680s, cross-referencing John Greaves's *Pyramidographia*. Rather than
> mystical prophecies, they show a rigorous, obsessive attempt to
> reverse-engineer the ancient Egyptian Memphis cubit and the Jewish sacred
> cubit using raw architectural dimensions. The missing folios detail
> calculations converting pyramid chamber heights into English inches, hunting
> for a mathematical link between ancient temple layouts and contemporary mint
> standards.
>
> He is buried a few hundred yards from where you stand. Find the wall he lies
> behind, and stand where the window is round.

*[Owner's text, two edits: "the first pyramid" → "the Great Pyramid", since
the Great Pyramid is not the first; and the last paragraph is the clue line,
new. "Where the window is round" is the rose window. Optional: a diary sketch
of the window, the owner's hand, as at stop 1.]*

*[TRUE: the papers, Greaves, the Memphis and sacred cubits, chamber
dimensions in English feet — all real. FICTION: "the missing folios" and the
link to mint standards. An edge between two true things, which is the only
kind we allow.]*

### After the gate — camera, stencil, Progress

### After — read once Progress is pressed

> Newton lies fifty feet behind this wall, in the nave against the choir
> screen. His monument was unveiled in 1731. He reclines beneath a globe of the
> heavens, and the globe rests on a pyramid. On the carving below him, boys
> handle a telescope and a prism, and one of them weighs the sun and the
> planets on a steelyard. The men who buried him put a pyramid and a balance
> on his tomb.
>
> He never saw Egypt. He took John Greaves's measurements of the King's
> Chamber, made in 1639, and from them fixed the cubit of Memphis at one
> thousand seven hundred and nineteen thousandths of an English foot. Twenty
> point six three inches. The figure accepted today is twenty point six. He was
> within a millimetre, from a book.

*[pause 1 s]*

> Three scorched pages of his own notes on the pyramid survive. They were
> burned, it is said, when his dog knocked a candle onto his desk. In December
> 2020 they were sold at auction for three hundred and seventy-eight thousand
> pounds, to a private buyer, and have not been seen since. Arthur's copy was
> made before the sale.

*[TRUE: everything to "not been seen since" — the monument, the steelyard,
1719/1000, 20.63, the Sotheby's sale and price, the dog. FICTION: "Arthur's
copy was made before the sale." One sentence.]*

**Question** *(before the after-text, or after — owner's call)*:
"From the working in the diary: how many thousandths of a foot?" → **1719**.
*[A number, read off the diary page that carries Greaves's chamber length and
Newton's rule of twenty cubits. Three guesses. The app says nothing about why

## Stop 3 · Location three → The Clock Tower

**Gate:** Parliament Square, east side by Bridge Street · 51.5008, −0.1258 ·
radius 50 m.
**Camera:** stencil of the **clock face** *[photo needed, or a geometric
placeholder: the face is a circle in a square with a known dial]*.

### Body

> The tower was built on the ashes. When they made the clock, the Astronomer
> Royal, George Airy, demanded that the first stroke of every hour be true to
> within a second, and had it checked against Greenwich by telegraph twice a
> day. It is kept to time by stacking old pennies on the pendulum. One penny
> gains it two fifths of a second a day.
>
> Airy is a name you will meet again. He chaired the commission that rebuilt
> the standards after the fire. He set this clock. He fixed the line from
> which the world measures time. And he is the man who decided what would not
> be heard.

*[TRUE: Airy's accuracy demand, the telegraph check, the pennies, the 1843
Standards Commission, the clock, the meridian. "The man who decided what
would not be heard" is a true fact stated early: the 1874 refusal. Nothing
invented; the last line is foreshadowing, not a claim.]*

**Question:** "What do they stack on the pendulum?" → **PENNIES**.

**On the way down to the river:** *[passing beat, optional camera]* the benches
along the Embankment have winged sphinxes for arms. They were put there in
1877, a year before the stone arrived.

## Stop 4 · Location four → Cleopatra's Needle

**Gate:** the pavement at the Needle · 51.5085, −0.1203 · radius 40 m.
**Camera:** stencil of the **obelisk and the right-hand sphinx** *[photo needed
from the pavement, obelisk and one sphinx in frame]*.

### Body

> Follow the river north until you reach the oldest thing in London.

*[Thutmose III, c. 1450 BC. The clue is short because the route is obvious
from the clock tower.]*

### After

> It was cut at Heliopolis thirty-four centuries ago and stood there for a
> thousand years before Cleopatra was born. Six men died bringing it through
> the Bay of Biscay in 1877. It was raised here on the twelfth of September
> 1878.
>
> Look at the sphinxes. They face the stone. Egyptian sphinxes face outward;
> they are guardians. These were set up the wrong way round, and nobody has
> ever turned them.
>
> Sealed in the pedestal under your feet is a jar. On the list of what it
> holds: twelve photographs of the best-looking English women of the day, a box
> of hairpins, a box of cigars, a razor, a railway timetable, a complete set of
> British coins, and a set of imperial weights.

*[pause 1 s]*

> Arthur has crossed out the coins.

*[TRUE: all of it, on the record, including the list. ⚠ "six men" — confirm
the number. FICTION: none. "Arthur has crossed out the coins" is a statement
about the diary, and the diary is ours.]*

**Question:** "Which way do the sphinxes face?" → **INWARD**, **TOWARDS IT**,
**THE STONE**. *[The diary word with the gap for this section: **_PHINX** →
S. Or take S from PENNIES? No — S from SPHINX; it is the better word.]*

Then Northumberland Avenue to Trafalgar Square.
