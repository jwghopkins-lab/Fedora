## Two things I got wrong, and they are mine

**1. I wrote the treatment from the history, not from `CAST.md`.** So the book
is missing two whole threads that the walk is built on.

- **Belsky is not in the treatment at all.** Parts 4, 15 and 16 — Mountbatten,
  Cunningham, the sixpence — serve nothing in the book. And `CAST.md` calls him
  "the strongest thread in the game."
- **The light thread is not in the treatment at all.** Parts 20, 21 and 23 —
  pavement light, gas lamps, electricity — serve nothing either.

That is six of twenty-three parts, a quarter of the walk, orphaned by a
document I wrote three turns ago.

**The fix is to change the book, not the walk.** The treatment jumps from 1918
straight to the present — a sixty-five year hole in a story about measurement
being legislated out of physical existence. Belsky fills it exactly: a sixpence
sealed into a bust in 1967, the year the Decimal Currency Act began killing the
coin; a jam jar of coins in a statue's leg in 1983, twelve days after the metre
stopped being a bar and became a property of light.

**And that last fact is the join.** The metre becoming light is where the
measurement thread and the light thread meet. The book currently has neither.

**2. The ending lands on nothing.** The final word is SOUTHAMPTON, and no part
of the walk ever mentions Southampton, or Clarke, or the figure of the Earth.
Players spell out a word that means nothing to them.

*Fix, and it is a craft fix not a structural one:* part 12's explainer, standing
on the terrace brass, should say that the shape of the Earth was computed from
bars like these, by a Royal Engineer who spent twenty-seven years at it **in a
town on the south coast** — and not name it. Then the word at the end is
recognition rather than information.

## Three new parts to fill the gaps

### NEW · Newton — 35 St Martin's Street *(beat F, the hinge)*

Newton lived at **35 St Martin's Street from 1710 to 1725**, off Leicester
Square. The house was demolished in 1913. The site is now the **Westminster
Reference Library**, and a plaque survives **inside**, at the top of the stairs
facing the entrance: *here stood the house of Sir Isaac Newton, in which he
lived from 1710 to 1727 and was visited by his friends Addison, Burnet, Halley,
Swift, Wren and other great men.*

It is free to enter and it is between Trafalgar Square and Covent Garden — so
it sits naturally around parts 18–19, exactly where the walk needs a beat.

**Why it is worth the detour:** the hinge of the whole story is that Newton did
pyramid metrology first, so the Royal Society's contempt for Smyth was unsafe.
Without this, that argument is made only in prose. With it, players stand in
the building where his house was.

*Costs:* opening hours, and it is indoors. Confirm both on the walk. Also
confirm the plaque's exact wording — sources give the dates as 1710–1727 on the
plaque and 1710–1725 in most biographies, and that discrepancy is itself
usable.

### NEW · A parish boundary mark *(beat I)*

Small iron or stone plates with initials and a date, set into walls at the
parish line. The physical residue of beating the bounds, invisible until
pointed at.

**Pending the field walk** — we do not yet know how many survive on St Martin's
Lane, Bedford Street or Maiden Lane. This is the highest-value unknown on the
list, because it would put the nettles chapter on the pavement.

### NEW · The Jewel Tower, Old Palace Yard *(beat B, and the modern collapse)*

One of two buildings that survived the 1834 fire. The **Standard Weights and
Measures Department worked in it from 1869 to 1938**, and left because **road
traffic vibration made precise measurement impossible.**

Two hundred metres from part 2. Act 1 currently runs Parliament Square →
Whitehall, and this is a short step the other way.

*Confirm on the walk:* is anything readable from outside, and does it earn a
part or just an explainer line.

**Three cut, three added. Back to 23.**

## Outstanding — too big to fill from here

### ⚠ Smyth has no object in London

He is the emotional centre of the story and there is nothing of him on the
route. He was born in Naples, worked at the Cape and in Edinburgh, and died at
Ripon.

**The only London candidate is Burlington House, Piccadilly** — the Royal
Society was there from 1857, so the 1874 rejection happened in that building.
It is roughly ten minutes off route, north-west of Trafalgar Square, and adding
it would unbalance Act 1 badly.

Three options, none obviously right:
1. Accept that Smyth lives in the diary and the explainers only.
2. Take the Burlington House detour and rebuild the Act 1 / Act 2 join.
3. Find a Smyth-adjacent object closer in. Nothing suggests itself.

### ⚠ 1884 has no object at all

The two dates are the strongest fact in the story and neither has a physical
London anchor. Greenwich is not on the route and cannot be.

Best available: **carry it in part 19's explainer**, at Mr Fogg's, where Verne
is already doing the work. That makes the fortnight an aside rather than a
beat, which undersells it.

### ⚠ Clarke and 1881

No London object. Accept explainer-only, and use the unnamed-town device at
part 12 so the ending can land.
